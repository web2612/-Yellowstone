Our Living Purpose

A Unified Vision for Environmental Conservation & Landscape Protection

"The Greater Yellowstone Coalition works with all people to protect the lands, waters, and wildlife of the Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem, now and for future generations."

Spanning Montana, Wyoming, and Idaho, the Greater Yellowstone Region harbors the continent’s premier intact wildlife assemblages. At its heart anchor Yellowstone National Park and Grand Teton National Park, enveloped by millions of acres of pristine Wilderness Areas protected under the Roadless Rule and sovereign public lands.

Through disciplined conservation planning, active ecosystem restoration, and uncompromising environmental advocacy, we counter rapid habitat fragmentation to secure ecological connectivity and highway wildlife crossings across the Rockies.

Answers to Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Yellowstone Conservation

The Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem spans more than 20 million acres across Montana, Wyoming, and Idaho. It is the largest nearly intact temperate ecosystem remaining in the Northern Hemisphere, providing irreplaceable habitat for grizzly bears, wolves, bison, and elk, while serving as the water tower for millions of downstream residents.

By combining specialized overpasses and underpasses with directional wildlife fencing, crossing structures channel migrating ungulates and carnivores safely across high-speed roadways. Proven projects show a reduction of wildlife-vehicle collisions by 85% to 90%, preserving animal migration while protecting drivers.

Enacted in 2001, the Roadless Rule protects roughly 58.5 million acres of inventoried roadless national forest lands from commercial logging and new road construction. Industrial special interests continually challenge this rule. We actively litigate and advocate to ensure these intact forest sanctuaries remain unfragmented.

We collaborate with more than 49 Native American tribes with ancestral ties to the Greater Yellowstone Region. Our partnerships focus on returning wild Yellowstone bison to tribal lands, honoring Indigenous treaty rights, and integrating traditional ecological knowledge into federal land management decisions.

As a certified 501(c)(3) environmental nonprofit, 85%+ of our funds directly support field biology, corridor acquisitions, water quality defense, non-lethal rancher conflict prevention tools, and grassroots education to safeguard the ecosystem for future generations.
