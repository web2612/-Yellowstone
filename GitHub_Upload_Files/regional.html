Landscape-Scale Jurisdiction

Tri-State Regional Focus: Montana, Wyoming & Idaho

Wild animals and watershed currents ignore political borders. Our cross-state strategy delivers coordinated public land management across three states and five national forests.

Cross-Border Conservation in Practice

The Greater Yellowstone Ecosystem spans more than 20 million acres, functioning as an integrated biological unit. Because management authority is split among three state fish and game agencies, two national parks, and multiple national forests, cohesive landscape conservation planning is vital to prevent habitat fragmentation.

1. Montana Wildlife & Watersheds

In southwestern Montana, broad agricultural valleys meet high alpine wilderness. Here, our focus centers on securing migration pathways where wildlife move between mountain summer ranges and valley winter habitats:

  • Highway Wildlife Crossings: Spearheading the construction of overpasses and underpasses on U.S. Highway 287 in our Madison Valley crossing project, saving thousands of elk and deer.
  • River Defense: Guarding the free-flowing character of the Yellowstone and Gallatin rivers against toxic algal blooms, industrial gravel mines, and private bank armoring.
  • Custer Gallatin Forest Protection: Enforcing the Roadless Area Conservation Rule across the Absaroka-Beartooth and Madison mountain ranges.

2. Wyoming Public Lands & Historic Migrations

Wyoming encompasses the southern and eastern anchors of the ecosystem, including Grand Teton National Park and the vast Bridger Teton National Forest:

  • Path of the Pronghorn & Red Desert Corridor: Protecting ancient ungulate migration routes that stretch over 150 miles between Grand Teton and the Red Desert.
  • Oil & Gas Lease Retirements: Negotiating permanent retirements of industrial drilling leases across the Wyoming Range to protect critical winter range.
  • Bison Co-Existence: Working with sovereign tribal councils to expand year-round habitat for wild herds through tribal bison repatriation partnerships.

3. Idaho Wildlife Habitat & Forest Connectors

Eastern Idaho forms the western frontier of Greater Yellowstone, featuring the fertile caldera of Island Park and the Caribou-Targhee National Forest:

  • Centennial Mountains Chokepoint: Guarding the only east-west forested bridge linking Yellowstone grizzly bears to the wider Northern Continental Divide Ecosystem in our Grizzly Bear Recovery and Corridor Connectivity initiative.
  • Henrys Fork Cold-Water Springs: Restoring spring-fed tributaries that sustain world-renowned wild trout fisheries.
  • Wintering Moose & Elk Buffers: Mitigating suburban sprawl and motorized winter recreation conflicts.
